ORIGIN & COERCION ARCHITECTURE

A brilliant Ukrainian nuclear physicist and chemist abducted by the KGB to spearhead the covert Soviet antimatter weapons programme beneath Chernobyl Reactor 4. His wife and child were held hostage in an undisclosed closed city to guarantee compliance. Surviving the April 26 explosion at ground zero, Yadro is driven by vengeance against the bureaucratic regime that weaponized science and destroyed his life.

⚛ ELEMENTAL MANIPULATION
Can alter and transmute atomic structures on the periodic table, strictly limited to elements whose chemical composition and crystal lattice he understands.
☢ IONISING RADIATION
Emits lethal bursts of gamma and neutron radiation at will, destroying living organic tissue and melting electronics in his immediate proximity.
🔊 SENSORY MOTIF: GEIGER SIGNATURE
Whenever radiation is emitted, an unmistakable Geiger counter clicking sounds as a terrifying auditory motif and final warning before death.
🧠 COGNITIVE DECAY (THE COST)
Continuous power usage triggers mental deterioration, amnesia, and blackouts. His mind and faculties only regenerate when he ceases power usage.
THE INTENTIONAL "GAP" // FORENSIC NEGATIVE CAPABILITY (LONG, 2007)

Two foundational questions are deliberately withheld from canon across all media: How could a mortal body survive the physical epicenter of a graphite reactor explosion? and How long can his cells survive before radiation self-destruction consumes him?

THE MAN IN THE LEAD APRON: PRE-1986 RECOVERED BIOGRAPHY

RECOVERED PERSONAL DIARY // DR. ANATOLY YADRO // CLASSIFIED TRANSCRIBED EXTRACT

The snow in Voronezh was three feet deep the morning Major Voronin knocked on our apartment door. He did not introduce himself as KGB. He did not need to. The black Volga idling on the curb with its exhaust smoking in the freezing dawn spoke with sufficient clarity.

"Pack one satchel, Doctor Yadro," Voronin said, taking off his leather gloves and warming his hands over Katya’s radiator. "Your country requires your chemistry. Specifically, your doctoral dissertation on relativistic sub-atomic particle collisions."

I told him I was a teacher. I told him my son Misha was recovering from scarlet fever. Voronin smiled—a cold, bureaucratic twitch of the lip. "Comrade Yadro, your family will remain in this flat under the benevolent protection of the State. Their comfort, their rations, their continued health will correspond exactly with your productivity at the Vladimir Ilyich Lenin Power Station."

Two days later, I was taken through the underground freight tunnel below Pripyat, past the civilian cooling pumps, down into Sub-Basement 4-B. They had excavated four stories beneath the foundation raft of Reactor 4 before the reactor hall above was even roofed. Concrete walls lined with cast lead blocks. In the center sat the magnetic compression ring—our machine. They called it the 'Antimatter Accumulator'.

For three years, I worked twelve hours a day beneath the hum of the turbines. Above my head, ordinary engineers and technicians monitored gauges and lived in sunny Pripyat flats, unaware that directly below their boots sat enough concentrated antimatter to crack the Eurasian plate.

Every letter I wrote to Katya was blacked out by Voronin's red pen. By March 1986, the pressure was unendurable. The construction above was rushed; the concrete was poor; the safety margins were slashed to meet the Five-Year Plan deadlines. I warned Voronin that the thermal flux from the reactor above was interfering with our magnetic containment coils. He told me that Soviet science does not accommodate bourgeois caution.

On the night of April 25, 1986, the alarms began shrieking through the ceiling pipes. The ceiling groaned like a dying beast. When the graphite roof collapsed and the core ruptured, the shockwave sheared our antimatter containment seals in a microsecond.

I remember no fire. I remember only light—a color that does not exist in nature, peeling apart the electrons of my hands, my chest, my bones. And then, the silence... followed by the sound of clicking.
